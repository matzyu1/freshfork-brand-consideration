# Data notes

## The dataset is synthetic
FreshFork Grill is a fictional UK quick-service restaurant and the survey is
generated by [`data/generate_survey.py`](../data/generate_survey.py). It is
fabricated, but not random: a latent-variable model gives it the structure a real
brand-perception survey has, so the analysis in [`src/`](../src) finds genuine
patterns rather than noise. The generator is documented in its own docstring; this
file records the data-quality decisions the cleaning and audit steps make, exactly
as they would on a real fieldwork export.

## Join integrity
The survey covers 2,377 respondents. The Demographics sheet has 12 columns; the
Brand Statements sheet has 34 statements plus respid. There are no duplicate
respondent IDs in either sheet and the two ID sets are identical, so merging the
sheets loses no respondents.

## Scale recoding
The 34 statement columns store the scale endpoints as text ("1 Completely
disagree", "10 Completely agree") alongside numeric values 2 to 9 — the way a
survey platform exports its anchors. All were converted to numbers 1 to 10.
"Don't know" was coded as missing rather than as a midpoint, because having no
opinion is not the same as holding a neutral one, and coding it as 5 would invent
a view the respondent did not express.

## "Don't know" responses
"Don't know" rates split cleanly by statement type. The highest are corporate and
citizenship claims: good employer (16.2%), a positive contribution to my community
(10.1%), an ethical and responsible company (9.8%), and reducing environmental
impact (9.8%). The lowest are everyday experience: friendly staff (0.5%), a place
I enjoy visiting (0.6%), makes me feel like a valued customer (0.7%), and a brand I
see and hear a lot about (0.7%).

The pattern is that people hold opinions about eating at FreshFork but not about
FreshFork as a company. This matters for strategy: messaging built on employer or
ethics credentials would be creating an opinion from nothing rather than shifting
an existing one. It also matters for the model — the corporate items are the ones
that lose the most respondents to missingness, which is why the driver base
(1,544 complete cases) is smaller than the full sample.

## Young families definition
Young families are households with a child under 10. The survey captures
children's ages in bands, so this maps to D8b_1 (under 1) through D8b_4 (7-9).
Bands D8b_5 (10-12) and D8b_6 (13-15) are excluded. This gives 936 young families
against 1,441 others. The comparison group still includes parents whose children
are all aged 10 or over, so it is not a "no children" group.

## D8 child-count cross-check
D8 records the number of children under 16 and caps its top category at "4+", with
a blank where the respondent has no children under 16, so the column mixes numbers
and text and is stored as a category. Rather than trust the label, it is
cross-checked against the six child age bands: nobody ticks more distinct bands
than they have children, and counts below the diagonal are expected because two
children can fall in the same age band. The cross-tab confirms the column behaves
as a child count and that blank means no children under 16.

## Straightlining
95 respondents (4.0%) gave an identical answer to all 34 statements. They split
into distinct groups: 33 answered 10 throughout, 32 answered 5 throughout, and 5
answered 1 throughout, with the remainder scattered.

The 5s are plausible: on a 10-point scale a respondent genuinely indifferent to
FreshFork could legitimately answer at the midpoint throughout. The 10s are harder
to defend, since they include perfect agreement on statements such as whether
FreshFork is a good employer, where 16% of the sample had no view at all.

All 95 were kept in the main analysis. The group mixes plausible indifference with
likely disengagement, the two cannot be separated cleanly, the total is under 4%
of the sample, and removing respondents on a judgement call needs stronger grounds
than this. They were flagged and the driver analysis was rerun without them as a
sensitivity check ([`05_drivers.py`](../src/05_drivers.py)); the factor shares move
by at most 1.4 points.

## Sample composition
The sample is 63% female and 37% male, and skews toward younger and middle-aged
adults: 30% are 35 to 44, 26% are 18 to 24, and only 6% are over 65. Mean age is
about 38.

No population weighting variables are provided, so the results describe this
sample rather than the UK population. The skew toward women aged 35 to 44 overlaps
heavily with the young families group, which is worth keeping in mind when reading
the subgroup findings.
